Just transactions, and people you owe or are owed by. No accounts, no balances, no budgets, no net worth — for tracking spending without setting up a full ledger.
Medium (default)
Everything in Basic, plus accounts (cash, bank, cards, loans, goals), account balances, net worth, and category budgets. The full app, minus envelope-style budgeting.
Everything in Medium, plus Ready to Assign — every account's money pools into one figure you assign to categories before spending it, classic envelope budgeting — and budget Rollover (carry unspent budget forward) and Overflow (redirect one category's overspend to another).
Money
Every place your money actually lives: cash, bank accounts, debit/credit cards, prepaid balances, and multi-currency accounts. Each can carry an optional minimum-balance floor that flags — never blocks — a transaction that would take it below that line. Its page shows its whole history, with an optional running balance after every entry, like a passbook.
Add one from the + button on the Accounts screen. Pick a type, a colour/icon, an opening balance, and (for a non-home currency) which currency it's in — that's locked in once the first transaction posts. On its page, Transfer in / Transfer out start a transfer with it already filled in — keep cash at home as its own Cash account and withdraw from it into your wallet like an ATM. Hold an account → Edit to change its name, colour and icon.
Correct a balance
When an account shows the wrong amount — say ₹100 in cash but your wallet has ₹75 — enter what you really have and the difference is saved as one "Correction" transaction. No made-up payment, and your history stays as it was.
Open the account and tap the pencil next to its balance. Corrections never count as money in or out, but each one in the last 30 days lowers the XPENC Score's Tracking habit — logging as you go keeps balances right on their own. Delete a correction to undo it.
Every money-in, money-out, transfer and lending movement you've logged, searchable and filterable by date, ISO week, account, or "linked" status.
Log one from the ➕ on any main screen. Long-press an existing one for Duplicate / Edit / Delete, or open it and tap the refresh icon to turn it into a recurring Auto rule pre-filled with its details.
Modes
A trip, a wedding, a festival — an event you switch ON. While it is ON everything you add joins it, tagged and limited the way you set it up, and the app shows only the modules it needs. Outside it, it is one card in Transactions and one slice in Stats, and budgets leave it out.
More → Modes → New mode, from Trip or Blank. Pick dates (it then starts and ends itself), a budget, the accounts and categories it may use, auto-tags, its own categories and tags, the tabs and modules to show, a linked group and a currency. On Add, switch "Part of …" off to keep one spend out. "Full app" in the bar over the tabs steps out without ending it. A transaction’s page moves it in or out of a mode. While ON the app is a sandbox: the home leads with what’s left to spend, every screen shows only the mode, Persons shows its people from zero, and each account what you allocated to it. Set limits (per day, per spend, per category), planned costs and a trip wallet in the editor; split bills with the mode’s people on Add; the switch off shows the summary to share.
Tracks who owes you and who you owe, kept separate from your own accounts — lending someone money isn't spending, and being repaid isn't money in.
Open a person to log a lend/borrow entry (with tags, if you like) or a repayment, and share their whole ledger as a PDF. Edit gives them a photo from your gallery. Hold a person for Edit, Link contact, Move to Settled, Archive or Delete (delete takes their whole history and puts the money back). Settings → Payment Support can turn on paying a person offline through the *99# USSD menu when there's no data signal.
Groups & Who owes whom
Split a shared expense across several people at once. Each group shows its overall balance, every member's net share, and the fewest payments that settle everyone up.
On the Group tab tap + to name a group and pick members. Open it to add expenses and see Who owes whom. Hold a group to Settle, Archive or Delete it.
Settled
People and groups whose balance is zero and that you've moved out of the way. They stay full, live rows — same history — and come back to their list on their own once money is owed again.
When a balance hits zero XPENC asks: Move to Settled, Archive, or Keep here. You can also hold a zero-balance row → Move to Settled, and hold a settled row → Move out of Settled.
A spending limit per category, tracked against what's actually been spent this period — Medium and Pro only, Basic has no concept of a budget at all.
Set one from a category's budget field. Pro adds Rollover (carry unspent budget into the next period) and Overflow (redirect one category's overspend into another) as optional per-budget settings.
Ready to Assign
Pro-only envelope budgeting: instead of a limit sitting on each category independently, every on-budget account's money pools into one "Ready to Assign" figure that you distribute across categories before it's spent.
Turn it on in Settings → Budgeting once you're on Pro. The More hub then shows a Ready to Assign tile with how much is still unassigned.
Auto (recurring rules)
Rules that post themselves on schedule instead of you logging them by hand: a fixed payment or money in, or a transfer between your own accounts — bank to cash, a credit card bill, into a savings goal, or an EMI into a loan.
New rule → Money out, Money in or Transfer, with a frequency (daily/weekly/biweekly/monthly/yearly), an optional promo price for the first few occurrences, and reminders before it's due. "Pay early" posts it today instead of waiting for the due date.
Every name typed into a transaction's payee field, grouped automatically — there's no separate payee list to maintain.
Type a payee while logging money in or out and it shows up here on its own, with its net flow (paid vs. received) and a link back to its full transaction history.
Savings goals track progress toward a target amount, optionally auto-funded from an account. Loans track what you owe — and, once you tell it the interest rate, tenure and start date, XPENC computes the EMI itself, splits every payment into interest vs. principal automatically (a plain payment or a recurring EMI, either way), and shows how much paying extra actually saves you in interest. A goal can also work like a pocket of your bank account: turn on "Show in Money in / out" on its page to pay or receive straight from it.
Add a goal or loan from the + on the Goals & Loans tab. For a loan, fill in the rate/tenure/start date to unlock the full tracking — leave them blank for a plain balance-only loan. "Make a payment" lets you backdate the date/time and shows the interest/principal/extra breakdown as you type.
Plan purchases before you make them — named lists (e.g. "Groceries") with items you check off, kept separate from your actual transactions until you decide to log them.
Create a list, add items, tick them off as you shop.

Backup & Restore
Manual backups plus scheduled auto-backups, with a choice of retention rule (keep the last N, or a time window). Restoring replaces the current ledger with a backup's contents — the safety net for switching phones or undoing a mistake.
Back up any time from this screen; auto-backups run on their own schedule once turned on. Long-press to batch-select several backups to delete at once.
Setup
Money in and out categories, each with its own icon and colour, picked from a searchable icon sheet. Tap one to see how much went to it — this month, this year or all time — split by subcategory, with every transaction behind the figure.
Save your whole category structure as a named template and switch between saved templates any time — switching never touches a transaction. The archive button lists archived categories to restore, or to delete one nothing has ever used.
Labels that cut across categories — a tag isn't tied to money in or out, so it can mark anything that shares a context (e.g. "Trip to Goa").
Tag groups bundle tags that always go together (e.g. "Work trip" = Travel + Meals) so you pick the whole set at once from the tag picker.

Savings rate
Share of money in left after money out over the last 90 days. 30% or more earns full points; 0% or less earns none.
Living within means
Of the last 3 full months, how many ended with money out at or below money in.
Debt load
Monthly debt obligations as a share of monthly money in — loan EMIs, credit card and pay-later dues, plus a third of what you owe people. 20% or less earns full points; 60% or more earns none.
Repayment discipline
A payment into every active loan in each of the last 3 full months, and no overdue amounts owed to people.
Emergency fund
Cash, bank, prepaid and savings-goal balances measured in months of average spending. 6 months or more earns full points.
Budget discipline
Share of your budgets still within their limit this period.
Tracking habit
Days with at least one entry in the last 30. 12 or more earns full points — the score is only as accurate as the ledger behind it. Each balance correction in the same 30 days takes away a quarter, since it means entries were missed.
