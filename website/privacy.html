Privacy Policy

Everything stays on your phone.

Effective date: 27 November 2026  ·  Applies to: XPENC for Android (com.yash.xpenc), v1.1.0+, and XPENC for iPhone, v1.6.4+  ·  Developer: Yash Patil  ·  Contact: feedback.yashpatil@gmail.com

The short version: XPENC stores everything on your phone and nothing anywhere else. The app has no server, no user accounts, no analytics, no crash reporting, no ads, and no third-party SDKs that collect data. On Android, release builds do not request the INTERNET permission, so the app is technically incapable of transmitting your data anywhere. On iPhone, where apps can't give up network access, XPENC's code makes no network requests.

Data the app stores — on your device only

When you use XPENC you create financial records:

  • accounts and their balances (cash, bank, cards)
  • transactions (income, expenses, transfers) with amounts, dates, categories and notes
  • budgets and budget alerts
  • persons and dues/loans you record against them
  • reminders and calendar entries
  • app settings

All of this is stored in a private SQLite database inside the app's private storage area, which Android sandboxes from other apps. It is never transmitted to the developer or to any third party. The developer has no way to see, access, or recover it.

Data the app collects or shares

None. XPENC collects no personal data, no financial data, no device identifiers, no usage analytics, and no diagnostics. Nothing is shared with anyone, because nothing ever leaves the device. If you pick a person from your contacts, that one contact's name, number and photo are copied into the app's local storage — the same on-device, never-transmitted storage as everything else here. The app's Google Play Data safety section accordingly declares no data collected, no data shared.

You can review each permission, and turn the optional ones on or off, in Settings → Permissions inside the app.

XPENC requests no SMS, location, storage, or internet permission (for the one exception, the opt-in beta build of Auto money tracking, see Bank-message capture). The microphone is used for nothing but voice add. (The final app package also contains VIBRATE, WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE — standard, install-time permissions the notifications library uses to deliver reminders on time — and an internal Android-library permission scoped to the app itself. None of them gives access to any of your data.)

On iPhone

XPENC for iPhone is the same app under this same policy: your data lives in the app's own private storage on the iPhone and nowhere else. iOS has no internet permission an app can drop, so the guarantee there is the code itself — XPENC contains no code that makes a network request. Links you choose to open (a UPI, PayPal or Venmo payment, an email to the developer) are handed to another app or your browser.

iOS asks for each of these only when you first use the feature, and you can change your answer in the iPhone's Settings:

  • Camera — to take a photo of a receipt.
  • Photos — to attach a receipt photo from your library.
  • Contacts — to import a phone number and photo for a person you pick.
  • Face ID — to unlock the app lock. XPENC never sees your face data; iOS only says whether the check passed.
  • Notifications — for budget alerts and reminders.

On iPhone, backups are kept inside the app, so deleting XPENC deletes them too. A backup leaves the app only when you share it — to Files, iCloud Drive or anywhere you choose. Like most iPhone apps, XPENC's data may be included in your iPhone's own iCloud or computer backup if you have that turned on; that backup is operated by Apple under your device settings and Apple's privacy policy, not by XPENC.

XPENC web and Connector

XPENC web (xpenc.in/web) keeps your data in that browser's own storage and, as it comes, talks to nothing but xpenc.in. It can go online only if you turn on Connector yourself, in Settings → Connector. Then, to keep it in step with XPENC on your phones, it signs you in with GitHub (through the Connector sign-in, run on Supabase) and keeps your backup — the same JSON file XPENC makes — in a private GitHub repository of yours, XPENC-BACKUP. If the backup it takes in from your phones is encrypted (Encrypt backups, 1.6.7 beta), it keeps that backup's key in the browser and encrypts its own uploads the same way; otherwise the backup isn't encrypted there. It also reads your Connector friends' usernames, to link people. Disconnect at any time; your data in the browser and on GitHub stays.

The Connector app (CONECTR) is a separate, open-source app with its own choices: it can keep your backups in your own Google Drive, in your own GitHub, or in ExoCloud, run by ExoSynk Labs, where they're end-to-end encrypted on your phone first. What it stores and where is set out in its own privacy notes. XPENC itself never talks to any of them.

Connector friends. On Android, if the separate Connector app is installed, XPENC can read the list of your Connector friends from it, on the phone, to link a person to a friend. The link — the friend's Connector id and username — is stored only in XPENC (and in its backups). XPENC still has no internet permission and sends Connector nothing.

Backups and exports

  • In-app backup/export (JSON and CSV): created only when you tap the button, and handed to the Android share sheet — you choose where the file goes (your files, your email, your cloud drive). Exported files contain your financial records in readable form, so treat them as sensitive. The developer never receives them.
  • Android system backup: like most Android apps, XPENC's data may be included in your device's own backup (for example Google's device backup tied to your Google account) if you have that enabled in Android settings. That backup is operated by Android/Google under your device settings and Google's privacy policy, not by XPENC.

OCR corrections (optional)

Settings → Message Capture → OCR corrections lets you test a payment-app screenshot against XPENC's on-device OCR and mark whether it read correctly. This does nothing unless you open that screen yourself.

  • Screenshots are processed on-device only — the image itself is never stored or included in anything you send, only the correction you choose to keep.
  • Tapping "Send" hands the extracted text — never the image — to your own email app or the Android share sheet, the same mechanism used for backup exports above. XPENC's own code makes no network call; you choose where the text goes, the same way you choose where an exported backup goes.
  • What's sent is a small JSON snippet: the payment app and country you labeled it with, the raw recognised text, the amount/direction/payee/ reference XPENC's parser guessed from it, and what (if anything) you corrected. No account balances, transaction history or other financial records are included.

Data retention and deletion

Your data stays on your device until you remove it. You can:

  1. delete individual records inside the app,
  2. clear the app's data in Android settings (Apps → XPENC → Storage → Clear data), or
  3. uninstall the app — Android deletes the app's private storage with it.

On iPhone, deleting the app removes all of its data, including backups kept inside it.

There is no server-side copy to delete, and no "account" to close.

Children

XPENC is a personal finance tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and it collects no data from anyone regardless of age.

Bank-message capture (BETA, opt-in build only)

Version 1.0 offered on-device scanning of bank SMS to pre-fill transactions; it was removed in version 1.1.0. The XPENC released on Google Play, F-Droid and GitHub requests no SMS permission.

It is coming back as Auto money tracking (Settings → Advanced Features, marked BETA). Only builds made specially to test it request READ_SMS: today that is one opt-in file on the GitHub beta pre-release, xpenc-beta-sms-arm64-v8a.apk, which you have to choose over the normal beta APK. In every other build the screen says it isn't available. Where it works:

  • it reads only messages from the bank senders you keep switched on (for example JD-HDFCBK-S), never a message from a phone number, and never a bank's adverts;
  • it only reads: XPENC never sends, changes or deletes a message, and does not listen for new ones in the background (no RECEIVE_SMS) — it reads when you open the app;
  • each payment it finds becomes a card in Review Inbox, stored with the rest of your on-device data; the message text itself is never transmitted.

Before a stable release or the Play Store version carries READ_SMS, it will be announced in the release notes, and this policy and the Play Store Data safety section will be updated before that release. Processing stays fully on-device.
